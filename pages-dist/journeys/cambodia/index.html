Cambodia

Battambang - Siem Reap - Phnom Penh

The story

BattambangSiem ReapPhnom Penh

01

Battambang

Battambang was the first city of our first international trip with the bois, so naturally it felt a little special. The city itself was quite industrial, to the point where even the person at our resort looked confused when we said we had come there for a vacation. Our stay was really nice though, and somehow the breads became one of the things we still remember from that place. We did not have a guide or a translator. We just stepped out and started walking around the city, figuring things out as we went. That probably made Battambang feel more memorable than it otherwise would have.

02

Siem Reap

Siem Reap was a bit of a roller coaster, but in the best way. Our stay was amazing, easily one of the best from the trip. We had planned to explore Angkor Wat on our own, but the managers at our stay suggested taking a guide because of how vast the temple area is. That turned out to be one of the best decisions we made. Our guide was friendly and gave us just enough history to understand what made each temple special without making it feel like a lecture. A special mention to our cab drivers too, who welcomed us after every temple with cold water and refreshing towels. In that heat, it felt like a luxury. Later, we explored the modern side of Siem Reap with restaurants, small shacks, great food and crazy vibes. We found one place we loved so much that we went back twice.

03

Phnom Penh

Phnom Penh felt like a complete contrast to the places we had visited before. Tall buildings, luxury cars, modern apartments and fancy cafes were everywhere. The city almost screams that it is rich, but at the same time it feels very diverse. You could see expensive cars passing through streets that still had a very local and traditional feel to them. There was a strange mix of old and new everywhere. Modern cafes and high rise buildings on one side, busy local streets and smaller shops on the other. It felt more fast paced and developed, but still had that rawness which made it interesting. Compared to Battambang and Siem Reap, Phnom Penh felt much more like a big capital city. More traffic, more movement, more people, and definitely a different energy altogether.
